# Research contributions

The replication follows the July 2024 preprint and its online appendix. The original authors introduced BASL and Bayesian scorecard evaluation. They ran a feedback simulation, compared methods on an independently randomized Monedo holdout, varied prior quality, studied missingness and varied loss given default. Our replication implements that work independently. Our research additions ask the following questions.

## In brief

* **Replication and its main extension.** We implemented BASL, Bayesian evaluation and the feedback simulation, then varied historical selection on two public cohorts. Under the hard cutoff in Taiwan, Bayesian evaluation reduced estimation error while BASL raised approved defaults in every fold. Giving the evaluator a random 5% of training-reject outcomes improved its risk estimates. Adding the same labels to ordinary scorecard training lowered approved defaults by 1.45 percentage points. [Study](selection-strength.md)
* **One idea across the extensions.** A missing outcome is worth learning only if it can change a lending decision. That idea decides which totals to request, how many outcomes an audit needs and when a loss-making pilot, such as the audit above, pays for itself.
* **Tests.** Each extension was locked before its external test where possible. Most refinements tied with or lost to simple pooled rates out of sample, and we report those results.
* **What is new.** The tools are standard; the contribution is their application to lending, the decision quantities and the dated empirical tests listed below.

## Questions, experiments and results

| New question | Experiment and result | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| When does reject inference work, and what can an audit improve? | We varied the approval slope and audited a random 5% of training rejects. Finite slopes target the same expected approval share. Ties at the Taiwan hard cutoff raise actual approvals to 67.3% from 63.7%. | At that cutoff, the audited prior ranks validation rejects more accurately (AUC 0.71 vs. 0.41). Ordinary scorecard training with the audit lowers the default rate by 1.45 pp. The initial AUC characterizes the evaluation prior. BASL fits a separate labeler in each round. [Protocol](selection-strength-protocol.md) · [Results](selection-strength.md) |
| Which measurements suffice for an entire menu of lending rules? | Apply the row-space criterion for estimable linear functions. For five distinct nested approval rules the four adjacent bands give a minimal basis for exact margin differences when each band carries independently unknown expected outcomes. | This gives a data-independent way to specify what a lender would want to measure before commissioning an audit. [Conditions, computation and examples](policy-information.md) |
| When can the evidence pay for itself? | A finite-horizon decision model compares approving one more marginal applicant with declining, both today and after learning from repayment. | The calculation prices an observation in terms of decisions it can still influence, conditional on how long risk persists. [Decision model and controls](exploration.md) |
| Can risk-group totals settle the lending decision? | Construct two finite populations with identical visible repayments and identical default counts within ten groups of hidden applicants. On Taiwan, every split admits opposite signs for the incremental margin, even with all ten **actual** hidden default counts disclosed in aggregate. In the first split two admissible completions give +0.900 and −0.888 pp. On Prosper the ten observed totals suffice to resolve the direction. A separate scenario substitutes rounded sums of prior probabilities. A five-cutoff decision game quantifies worst attainable regret relative to the best of those five cutoffs in each world. | Knowing the risk of a group can leave the risk of applicants crossing a lending threshold undetermined. [Counterexample and minimax results](identification.md) |
| How many individual outcomes would resolve the sign? | Randomly sample hidden borrowers on whom the 30% and 50% policies disagree. On Prosper a sequential exact audit requires 14.46 of 160 outcomes in expectation under the retrospective population and about 153 near break-even. | The lender can budget for an unresolved audit. The 5% error guarantee applies within the fixed cohort, under the specified random audit and financial scenario. [Protocol, proof and exact distributions](decision-audit.md) |
| Does recovering the correct metric on old applicants improve forecasts for new ones? | 12 calendar forecasts on Prosper. Bayesian evaluation reduces historical ABR estimation RMSE from **0.79 to 0.26 pp**, but next-quarter RMSE rises from **2.08 to 2.36 pp**. Dropping any one quarter preserves the ranking by squared error. | A good correction for selective labels and a good forecast through time are distinct achievements. The direction of the historical error can offset cohort changes. [Quarterly protocol and data](vintages.md) |
| How wrong can our assumptions about the rejects be before a policy expansion loses its case? | Compare a 30% incumbent with 35–50% approval targets. On validation, a 30→50% expansion keeps a nonnegative worst-case conditional expected gain up to reject-odds multipliers **1.85–2.30 in Taiwan** and **13.19 on Prosper**. Without restrictions on missing labels a zero-downside rule retains the 30% incumbent in both. | The lender can identify the applicants on whom the two policies differ and the assumptions needed to support expansion. This is an application of partial identification and sensitivity analysis. The intervals concern the stated validation population and cash-flow scenario. [Policy bounds and frozen-cutoff audit](bounds.md) |
| What can an actual randomized credit offer establish when some customers decline? | In the Philippine analytic extract, 650 of 891 offer recipients and 4 of 222 controls took loans. Standardizing over the two score windows, extending offers to both windows versus only the high window raises funding by **8.02 pp** and lender cash margin by **4.19 PPP dollars per applicant** before costs. | A trial can inform an offer policy while leaving the repayment outcome under universal take-up partly unknown. A high-window prior predicts 0.5 material principal shortfalls among 50 funded low-window borrowers; two occurred. Those rare counts are inconclusive. [RCT sources, identification and uncertainty](rct.md) |
| Which hidden labels are worth measuring for a given policy? | At a budget of 50 outcomes, sampling among historical rejects eligible for the candidate 30% policy reduces Taiwan audit RMSE from **2.01 to 0.86 pp**, versus draws from all historical rejects. | Concentrating measurements on applicants who enter the decision can improve precision. The budget fixes the number of labels; the financial cost of exploratory loans could differ. [Results](../results/audit_summary.csv) |
| Can an expected loss on one loan buy enough information to justify its cost? | Derive the one-pilot break-even condition, quantify how timing and persistence limit the value of repayment, and compare with continuous learning. Heterogeneous applicants admit sharp bounds even if the bank knows only their individual risks. | The decision-theoretic value of information is established. Our extension specializes it to lending, gives a computable ceiling and subjects the rule to a chronological replay. [Theory](information-value.md) · [Dependence bounds](pilot-dependence.md) · [SBA evidence](pilot-replay.md) |
| Can the bank choose useful test loans in advance, and can the signal repay a real loss? | Transfer to SBA 7(a) and Bondora. Evaluate forecast rankings and policy value, then trace the outcome of a fixed draw separately from branches identified afterward. | A selected region can contain a strongly profitable contingent action while the model still ranks future successes poorly. Actual implementation at a lender requires separate field evidence. [Targeting](pilot-targeting.md) · [Field evidence](observed-pilot-evidence.md) |
| What distinguished the two Estonian cases before funding? | Compare all selected pilots with mature segment history, then price the pilot and future loans at their own principal. Freeze the rule and test both recorded cash and strict information-driven recovery in two later calendar windows. | A cheap pilot limits the cost of learning. Profit still depends on whether the lender has a private signal and on what it would do without it. [Profiles](pilot-profiles.md) · [Sensitivity](pilot-signal-sensitivity.md) · [Holdout](pilot-holdout.md) |
| Does the lender need the pilot’s own outcome once it sees other loans? | A model prices observing one loan before issue. On 48 future portfolios the ordinary update and shared peer outcomes improved boosting. With the common feed held fixed the pilot’s own label gave a Brier improvement of −0.000097 (95% block interval −0.000469 to +0.000262). | An observation can shift a forecast while leaving the same action optimal. None of the 19 eligible pilots was worth funding under the fitted rule. [Dated design](pilot-signal-ml-protocol.md) · [Model and results](pilot-signal-ml.md) |
| Why did a historical rate beat boosting? | Decompose realized Brier error, replay the fitted tree at a different country rate and lock a frequency-anchored correction before testing the last four mature vintages. | The local rate tracked the Finnish risk decline. New public records changed one subsequent lending action, and the individual pilot changed zero. [Diagnosis](frequency_diagnosis.md) · [Fresh test](frequency-test.md) |
| When can the bank rely on a local rate? | Trace a Dutch startup forecast to the loans that actually supplied its labels. Transfer one prespecified shrinkage rule to a different lender and source, then test both the average forecast and its advance local-versus-broader choice. | The rule improved on always using sparse local history. The outside-state benchmark matched its lending decisions at both tested thresholds. [Dutch dates](dutch-frequency.md) · [Derivation](local-trust.md) · [External test](local-trust-bank.md) |
| Can a lender spot a fall before annual outcomes mature? | Convert serious payment trouble in younger, age-matched cohorts to a year-one risk forecast. Derive an observable warning threshold. Lock the calculation before scoring actual scheduled instalments from a separate bank. | Six of seven Dutch warnings were correct when measured against the later portfolios, three of them only because the conversion undershot the cohorts it measured. It changed Czech lending decisions while the other-region benchmark retained a lower forecast loss. [Rule and evidence](early-warning.md) |

Karlan and Zinman conducted the Philippine credit experiment. We used their public data to evaluate lending policies and repayment assumptions.

## Established methods behind the extensions

Several extensions apply standard tools to the lending problem. The contribution is the application, the decision quantities and the empirical tests.

| Extension | Established method | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Value of one pilot loan | Expected value of sample information | Raiffa and Schlaifer (1961), *Applied Statistical Decision Theory*; Howard (1966), *IEEE Transactions on Systems Science and Cybernetics* 2(1) |
| Totals that identify policy differences | Estimability of linear functions through the row space | Linear algebra; [Strang](policy-information.md) |
| Identical totals, opposite decisions; policy bounds | Partial identification and minimax regret | Manski, cited in [identification](identification.md) and [bounds](bounds.md) |
| Reject-odds multiplier Γ | Odds-ratio sensitivity models | Rosenbaum (2002), *Observational Studies*; Tan (2006), *JASA* 101(476) |
| Local versus broader rate, w = T/(T+V) | Credibility weighting and empirical-Bayes shrinkage | Bühlmann (1967), *ASTIN Bulletin* 4(3); Efron and Morris (1973), *JASA* 68(341) |
| Six-month to year-one conversion | Development factors in chain-ladder reserving; vintage curves | Mack (1993), *ASTIN Bulletin* 23(2) |
| Philippine offers | Randomized credit scoring | Karlan and Zinman (2011), *Science*; [analytic file](rct.md) |

## Scope and attribution

The public Taiwan and Prosper cohorts contain funded borrowers. Masking their repayment outcomes lets us measure error under a known approval rule. The Philippine trial also records who took up a randomized offer, but only eight funded accounts had a principal shortfall above one peso. These datasets differ in their products, follow-up periods and outcome definitions. We report each separately. [Data and definitions](sources.md) · [Philippine trial](rct.md)

The Dutch early-warning analysis was exploratory. The Wells Fargo pooling test and the Czech early-payment test each used a rule fixed before scoring that external dataset. [Analysis plan](analysis-plan.md)
