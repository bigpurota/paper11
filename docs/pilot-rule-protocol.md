# Locked test of a pilot rule with loan amounts

## Decision made before inspecting the new outcomes

The two successful Estonian pilots and the unsuccessful February 2019 switch are discovery cases. We had seen their outcomes when designing this rule. The rule uses loan amounts because the lender sacrifices one particular principal to acquire information about a portfolio of different principals. Country, customer status or risk grade can define a population for estimating risk. A country name, a calendar year, a particular principal and a particular number of past loans do not trigger selection.

At origination let A be the pilot principal, q its segment's estimated event probability, r = +0.10 the repayment margin and d = −0.45 the default margin per unit principal. The shared-risk prior has strength 20. We keep the old rule's universe: the pilot's expected direct scenario margin must be negative. Follow-up choices are lend to the available group or decline it. Both strategies use the same ordinary public update, and can use at most 25 later loans. The baseline continues to use the fixed current risk and 25 equal-sized loans when deciding whether to pilot.

The new rule forecasts future principal by taking the first up to 25 originations in each of the **previous 12 complete calendar months** in the same segment, including empty months, and averaging their total principal. This represents 12 possible future portfolios, observable at issuance. To model ordinary risk updates at the date the pilot becomes informative, use all earlier origin/update pairs from the same portfolio whose update date has arrived, across segments, equally weighted by segment-date observation. Starting at eight distinct mature origin dates, transfer realized public log-odds changes to today's prior and recenter their mean probability at q. Until then hold q fixed. No performance data from the evaluation drive either the forecast parameters or selection threshold.

At each possible public risk u, compute the additional margin per unit from the two feasible post-pilot choices under a beta prior of strength 20. Average over u and multiply by forecast future principal. Add the pilot's direct expected margin in money. Issue the pilot exactly when the sum is strictly positive (numeric tolerance 1e−12 in units of pilot principal). The observation can change the decision in either direction. Realized actions at the follow-up date use the actual then-available common risk estimate and the actual pilot signal. The old and new rules share these actions.

Separately forecast the probability of **recovering a direct loss by changing the next decision**. A pilot default must move the action from funding to declining. Conditional on that default, approximate each historical monthly portfolio by its actual count and total exposure, with equal principal within that month. Integrate the beta-binomial probability that avoided losses on those loans exceed the pilot loss. Average over months and public risks. This forecast is for the fixed two-payoff scenario. Its transport to real cumulative cash is an empirical question. The probability is a ranking diagnostic, with no separately tuned cut point.

## Evaluation

The new evaluation archive or time period and its loan/label/cash definitions are fixed in the [holdout protocol](pilot-holdout.md). No policy selection or tuning uses its outcomes. A separate public commit records this rule before the new outcome run; this retrospective historical holdout is distinct from a live bank trial.

The primary monetary endpoint is the average incremental **recorded cash** per eligible opportunity, in source currency at actual loan sizes, comparing the new policy with both the previous fixed rule and skipping pilots. Both comparisons use identical borrowers and decision dates. A skipped pilot adds zero. A secondary scenario uses the same +0.10/−0.45 payoffs at actual loan sizes. Report selected counts, actual switches, strict recoveries, coverage, and precision of the strict-recovery forecast across all eligible opportunities. Assess discrimination with tie-adjusted AUC and average precision against the strict event's prevalence. When either class is empty report the ranking measure as unidentifiable. Resample four consecutive calendar quarters, jointly over segments, with 2,500 circular-block draws, seed 26011; report the number of estimable draws. Gain supported on this holdout requires positive lower 95% bounds for both paired monetary contrasts. Evidence of advance strict-event ranking requires both classes and a lower AUC bound above 0.5. Any ablations keep the same cases, use the declared components, and have descriptive status. No changes after the test are evaluated as confirmed wins.
