# When to trust a local default frequency

The eight Dutch Bondora forecasts for pilots dated October 2023–May 2024 are discovery cases. A small, slowly maturing early book and a later expansion motivated this rule. We publish the lender, dates, weights, comparators, endpoints and uncertainty calculation before scoring the new US archive. We retain the protocol and all outcomes regardless of which rule wins.

## One rule for two lenders

At the forecast date let n denote the number of mature loans in the local history and D its measured events. Let r count local originations over the equally long recent calendar window whose outcomes are not used in the forecast. The local frequency is L=(D+0.5)/(n+1). The broader rate G=(sum of other areas’ events +0.5)/(sum of other areas’ measured loans +1) excludes the focal area. Require a nonempty donor history. Where n=0 set the operational forecast to G and report cold-start coverage separately.

Estimate an across-area variance T using only other areas with at least 20 mature loans each. Compute the ordinary sample variance of their equally weighted smoothed rates and subtract the mean of their estimated sampling variances L_j(1−L_j)/(n_j+1). Truncate at zero. Use T=0 if fewer than three donors meet the support condition. All measured other-area loans still enter G. Discount local precision for expansion by using n_eff=n × min(1,n/max(1,r)). Set V=L(1−L)/(n_eff+1), w=T/(T+V) for n>0, and w=0 at cold start. The primary forecast is w·L+(1−w)·G. At w>0.5 a prespecified hard selector uses local; at a tie or below it uses broader. A comparator repeats the calculation without the expansion discount (n_eff=n). These numeric conventions and priors are fixed across datasets. The expansion discount is a modeling assumption. Future data may contradict it.

Describe the rule in the known Bondora cases using country, the existing mature 12-month window and the following 12 completed issue months before the forecast. The Dutch forecast retains its established 13-month horizon to the next first-25 book. Use no current or eventual Dutch outcomes to set a weight at issue. Those eight books cannot validate how the rule was designed.

## Independent bank and chronology

Use US **Wells Fargo Bank, National Association**, identified solely by numeric BankFDICNumber 3511, on SBA 7(a) loans. Use the 30 September 2026 approval extracts for fiscal years 1991–1999, 2000–2009 and **2010–2019**. The last archive has not been used in the previous project tests. The external test includes all nonempty eligible state/quarter portfolios from **2015 Q1 to 2019 Q3** inclusive in the 2010–2019 approval file. Use ProjectState in the 50 states and DC, present first disbursement on or after approval, positive original gross approval, and complete three-year observation by the snapshot. Exclude charge-off dates before disbursement. The event is a recorded charge-off by the third anniversary of first disbursement. If a terminal charge-off code contradicts a missing date, disclose and handle the unresolved event explicitly. No borrower names, addresses or loan-level files are published. [Official source catalog](https://data.sba.gov/dataset/7a-504-foia) · [Bank identity](https://banks.data.fdic.gov/bankfind-suite/bankfind/details/3511)

For forecasting quarter t, use loans disbursed in quarters t−24 through t−13 inclusive for local and other-state mature counts. The 12 following completed disbursement quarters t−12 through t−1 supply only volume. Lock the prediction before the first loan of quarter t. Target the current state-quarter's funded vintage with the same three-year event definition used in the historical count. Use no other lender's experience in the broader comparator. Overlapping historical windows are permitted. This is an external transfer of the trust rule to geographical areas within a single commercial bank. The change in horizon, product, event, and unit of geography limits direct comparisons with the Dutch pilot replay.

## What counts as success

On state-quarters with both local and outside support, average individual Brier loss within each state-quarter by **loan count**, then weight the evaluated state-quarters equally. Compare the same outcomes under local, broader, hybrid, hard choice and no-expansion-discount forecasts. Report dollar-weighted loss inside the books and loan-count-weighted aggregation across books as sensitivities. Give the data coverage, a separate operational result including cold starts, and summaries by quarter. Within w>0.5 and w≤0.5 report how frequently local was closer, ties and the mean of broader Brier minus local Brier. The hard selector’s ability to identify where local is preferable is a separate question from predictive improvement obtained by averaging the two forecasts.

For the primary hybrid's two contrasts use 2,500 circular moving-block resamples of four consecutive calendar quarters, aligned across states, seed 26011. Confirm superiority in this test only if both paired 95% lower bounds, local minus hybrid and broader minus hybrid, exceed zero. Evaluate lending above zero expected margin with fixed scenarios +10%/−45% and +3%/−50% of gross approval on a non-event/event respectively. Report how often actions differ and signed portfolio margin differences from both policies on the same books. Outcomes come from issued loans. These scenario values do not observe actual Wells Fargo profit, prices, funding costs or the effect of the SBA guarantee. No parameter, time window, lender or primary endpoint will be reselected from the test.
