# One pilot on Bondora European consumer loans

## Design fixed before scoring the test outcomes

Use the July 20, 2021 snapshot of loans issued on Bondora, supplied by the named archival mirror. Each episode is one country, new/returning credit customer group and calendar month. Starting January 2013, consider the first funded loan in each nonempty cell as the candidate pilot. Use the loan number to order ties on an origination date. The initial decision uses the latest 12 fully mature monthly cohorts of the same group, requiring 50 past loans. The signal is a recorded default dated within 12 calendar months of origination. A common refresh reaches both policies before the beginning of month 13 after the pilot, when even the last pilot-month loan has aged a year. The refreshed rate uses the latest 12 mature months excluding the pilot cohort. If this window is empty, retain the initial rate.

For a binary economic benchmark retain the earlier project's +0.10 performing margin and −0.45 default margin per unit principal. Estimate the event frequency with one-half event and one-half non-event. Plan for 25 subsequent opportunities and a beta prior of strength 20. A pilot is eligible when it has negative predicted direct margin. Select it only when its additional predicted decision value covers that loss. At follow-up fund or pass on **up to** the first 25 funded loans to arrive during that month. Keep episodes with fewer or zero arrivals. The comparison strategy acts on the same updated common information without the pilot signal. The initial forecast holds current risk fixed, including over the intervening public refresh. The forecast and all policy settings are frozen before scoring.

Each episode is a separate experiment conditional on an external book of ordinary lending results. Adjacent months share historical windows and borrowers may return. Totals across episodes describe the stated paired rule with that external information supply.

All portfolios are replayed on historically funded loans. Our principal endpoint applies the scenario to one-year defaults for which both cash outcomes are prespecified. In parallel, compute recorded cumulative principal payments plus interest and penalties, less original principal, up to the report date. Separate normalized equal-exposure returns from the supplementary portfolio of actual loan amounts. We retain every outcome irrespective of status. The primary cohort allows at least 36 calendar months from the end of the follow-up origination month to the snapshot. A sensitivity extends coverage to the complete follow-up months with at least 12 months' seasoning. Cash may still arrive after the snapshot. Checks on the published original maturity date distinguish loans already past contractual maturity. There are no monthly collection histories or reported institution-level funding costs in this margin.

Count a strict repayment of the pilot's loss only when the candidate's realized margin is negative, the observed signal changes the rule's funding action, and the incremental margin on that same later portfolio strictly exceeds the direct loss. Report the count for the predicted selection and separately for forced pilots on all eligible opportunities. The first chronological example, if one exists, is descriptive. Compute four-quarter circular moving block sensitivity intervals holding each quarter's country/customer groups together. Use all prespecified episodes even when the rule chooses to skip.

## Source and outcome timing

The archived July 20, 2021 loan tape contains 179,235 uniquely numbered loans from Estonia, Finland, Spain and Slovakia. Origination dates span February 28, 2009 through July 19, 2021. The source reports its cut date in every loan record. The held-out risk rule uses only origination date, country, new-customer status and mature historical default dates. Loan number orders a daily batch when several accounts have the same recorded day. Its role is to approximate the order of funded loans with a date-level tape. There is no intraday application log. No identifier appears in the submitted outputs. [Bondora public portfolio statistics](https://goandgrow.eu/en/public-statistics/) · [Archived July 2021 release, version 1](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/sid321axn/bondora-peer-to-peer-lending-loan-data/versions/1)

There were 203 primary opportunities and 5,020 follow-up positions. Exactly 100 opportunities had negative predicted direct margins. Two qualified for a pilot. Neither outcome changed the follow-up action, even when forcing a pilot in every eligible episode. The mean incremental normalized cash margin of the selective strategy was +0.0070 per eligible episode on unit individual principal. Both loans directly earned positive cash margins, so all of this observed gain came from the loans themselves.

The expanded 12-month-seasoned set contained 347 opportunities. Of 228 eligible episodes, the rule selected ten pilots. Three realized outcomes switched the next decision. All three were defaults among repeat Estonian borrowers. Under the prespecified binary payoff, none of the switches covered its pilot's loss. On average the selected policy changed the binary margin by −0.0068 per eligible episode (4-quarter block interval −0.0222 to +0.0030). In the July 2021 cumulative payment tape the same three switches appeared to cover the pilots' cash shortfalls. A later tape tests that last observation. ## What the same borrowers had actually paid three years later

After inspecting the July 2021 cash results we sought a later tape to check collection lag. The May 24, 2024 release contains 389,970 accounts. Matching the stable loan ID and loan number, and checking origination date and principal, linked 179,228 of the original 179,235 records. All 347 pilot positions and all 8,238 continuation positions matched. Each record in the later file carries the May 24 report date. A subsequent mirror advertised in September 2025 retained exactly the same collections for these historical borrowers. We use the dated 2024 tape. Neither pilot choice, signal, comparison portfolio nor contingent action was re-estimated. [Archived May 2024 release, version 24](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/marcobeyer/bondora-loans/versions/24) · [All later-ledger comparisons](../results/lender_pilot_followup.csv)

For the October pilot the estimated risk at origination was 18.73%. It had a predicted direct margin of −0.0030 per unit and a forecast net gain including the signal of +0.0352. Before the follow-up the refreshed public estimate had fallen to 15.76%. Because break-even risk was 18.18%, the no-pilot bank would fund the group. The pilot's recorded default pushed estimated risk to 19.77% and the informed rule declined. The pilot itself had face value €530. Recorded principal of €205.91 plus interest and penalties of €57.09 left a €267.00 cash loss. The tape separately records €324.09 principal written off and zero principal still due. The same identity holds for both other defaulted pilots using their recorded write-offs and zero balances. A “Repaid” platform status can therefore accompany a negative investor cash margin after a write-off.

The three portfolios were actually financed in history. The resulting repayments let us calculate the difference the specified switch would have made. Bondora's dataset does not say that it collected the first label to guide a subsequent lending decision or actually rejected the 25 loans. The July 2021 example set was identified in a secondary seasoning window. All ten pilots had been chosen prospectively by the same fixed rule; the three switches came from a single country/customer group. The main seasoning cohort showed no such switch.

With equal-size positions, the late-snapshot net gains on the three switches were +3.838, +2.414 and −4.857 units of single-loan principal. Using actual face amounts, all ten selected pilot episodes produced +€16,334.80 in aggregate, median +€140.70 and range −€8,672.42 to +€15,944.29. The policy skipped the other 218 eligible pilot opportunities. Per eligible opportunity the 2024 cash estimate was +€71.64 (95% four-quarter block interval −€63.21 to +€223.32). The corresponding mean on equal-size loans was +0.0093, interval −0.0383 to +0.0474. Positive cash observations in two episodes do not establish that the selection rule generalizes.

**Outstanding claims matter.** At the May 2024 cut, 9, 12 and 10 of the respective 25 loans were marked Repaid. If the declined October portfolio subsequently collects another €15,944.29, the measured advantage drops to zero, holding the pilot fixed and ignoring discounting. This is 53.74% of its remaining principal. For November the analogous share is 39.17%. Valuing all remaining portfolio principal at par eliminates both positive differences. Residual interest, recoveries, funding, capital costs, servicing and time value also affect lifetime economics. The measured observation is additional cash collected by the cut date, before such adjustments.
