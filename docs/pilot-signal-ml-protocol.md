# Locked test of what one pilot adds to a lender’s ordinary information

The March 2021 Finnish case and the two loans originally selected are discovery cases. This follow-up was designed after seeing them. Before computing the new paired predictions, this commit fixes the time windows, information sets, model, issue rule, comparisons and interpretation. Only origination dates, countries, other contract fields, the previously published results and source metadata were used to choose the new window.

## Dates and units

Use the pinned November 2026 Bondora workbook. Each observation is one country and one origination month s. Choose its first funded loan, using issue-time ordering, as the pilot. Use the first up to 25 loans in the same country in s+13 as the continuation, retaining empty portfolios. A first-year default is visible at the start of month s+13 and the continuation's first-year default outcomes at the start of s+26. Estimate the original risk q from the previous fully mature year of cohorts with at least 50 measured labels; retain the old Jeffreys estimate. The ordinary refresh uses the same windows as the [previous experiment](pilot-holdout.md). Under the primary disclosure assumption both lenders also see all measured same-country, same-month first-year peer outcomes by s+13, **excluding the pilot**. A prespecified sensitivity hides those peers from both. Actual lender data-sharing arrangements are unknown. Country is consistently present across the grading change.

Freeze all fitted models and the distribution of possible updates at the start of October 2022. Training rows require s+26 at or before October 2022, a known pilot signal and a nonempty, fully measured continuation first-year target. Include all initial profit signs. No cash, remaining balances or subsequent liquidation information from the November 2026 workbook enters training. The test consists of **all October 2022–May 2024 origins**. Its continuation months (November 2023–June 2025) lie outside earlier scored continuation windows. Some early test pilots may have been continuation loans in an earlier experiment. Report exact overlap where identifiable. Also report the fixed November 2023–May 2024 subset, whose pilot origins are later than the previous scored continuations. These are new paired experiments on a familiar archive. Mere possession of a workbook is distinct from prospective collection of field evidence.

## Predictors and forecast comparison

The learner is scikit-learn's histogram gradient boosted regressor. Fit squared error, 80 iterations, learning rate 0.05, seven maximum leaves, minimum 20 rows per leaf, L2 penalty 5, no early stopping, random seed 1103. Every usable training country-month carries equal weight. Require at least 80 training rows and 12 distinct origin dates. Treat unseen country levels as all zero in the training one-hot encoding and use the model's handling of missing numeric contract fields. Freeze preprocessing on training rows. Clip predicted probabilities to [0, 1]; clip only for evaluating logs at 1e−12. The repayment model target is the subsequent portfolio's fraction of original principal attributable to first-year defaults. Neither final cash nor a test label chooses a hyperparameter.

1. Issue-time predictors O are country, q, log(1+number of mature historical loans), log(1+pilot principal), original interest rate and term, log(1+average first-25 principal over the preceding 12 complete months) and the corresponding average count. Empty historical months have zero exposure.
2. P adds the ordinary refreshed default risk at s+13. C adds log(1+number of measured peers) and the pooled peer PD (peer defaults + 20 × refreshed PD)/(peer count + 20). These are disclosure-time variables. Estimate separate O, P and C continuation models for ablations.
3. Fit a continuation model on (C, pilot default Y) and a separate predictor of Y from C on exactly the same training rows. For a given public context score **the same fitted continuation model** twice, with Y set to zero and one, yielding m0 and m1. Let π be the estimated chance of pilot default. Without the label use m=(1−π)m0+πm1; with it use mY. Repeat this matched comparison replacing C with P for the no-peer sensitivity. The independently trained C-only model provides a second out-of-time check on predictive performance.

Evaluate all nonempty, complete test portfolios with known pilot outcome using the principal-weighted individual Brier score (1−d)p²+d(1−p)² within the portfolio; average across matched episodes, and report coverage. Primary predictive contrast is Brier(m) minus Brier(mY), positive if the actual label helps beyond the shared information. Also report the difference against the independently trained common-only model, forecast changes across the O/P/C ladder, and the paired change in log loss in bits. For a negative control replace the real Y at disclosure with a draw independent of the actual continuation, conditional on the fitted π. Compute its expected loss exactly from m0, m1 and π. This checks the contribution of matching an outcome to its own episode. It does not identify a causal effect by randomization.

## Pricing an unseen label and testing the decision

At issue, neither lender knows the actual peer outcomes, future public update or pilot label. Integrate over paired historical public refresh and peer updates in the frozen training sample. Use all training analogs from the same country if at least 12, otherwise all training rows. Transfer each analog's two log-odds differences (refresh minus original, peer-smoothed minus refresh) to the current q, retain that analog's peer count and give the joint contexts equal weights. The current contract fields are shared across the two branches. In each state define g(p)=0.10−0.55p. The incremental decision value per euro of future exposure is

    (1−π) max(0,g(m0)) + π max(0,g(m1)) − max(0,g(m)).

Multiply the mean by the forecast future exposure. Add the pilot's expected direct margin using its own principal and the mean π. Evaluate funding on the original eligible population (g(q)<0). Select a loss-making pilot if its modeled direct margin per euro is below −1e−12 and its total expected gain per euro of pilot principal exceeds +1e−12. A zero value means that learning cannot improve the current two-action decision under these assumptions. It need not mean the forecasts are identical. Disclosure-time actions use the usual positive-margin cutoff with numerical ties declined. Empty portfolios have zero changed loan actions.

At the common archive cutoff, compare the ML selector to skipping pilots, and to the earlier amount-aware rule reapplied to the same country-months. **Hold the downstream matched ML actions fixed** in these comparisons so the paired difference measures the choice of whom to fund. Report the pilot's own cumulative payments less principal, the cash from changed follow-up actions, their sum per eligible opportunity, and strict loss recovery. Scenario margins (+10%/−45% at actual original principal) are secondary. Never interpret an unfinished cash position as a final return. Report missing outcomes and identification of policy contrasts even if neither policy funds. Monetary confirmation requires positive lower bounds against both controls and complete required policy outcomes. Forecast confirmation requires a positive lower bound for the primary paired Brier improvement with complete required labels on nonempty forecastable portfolios. Use 2,500 circular moving-block draws of four contiguous calendar quarters, paired across countries, seed 26011. Report estimable draws, skip intervals when the window spans at most four quarters, and show country-level descriptive checks. Twenty origin months provide only seven quarters. Country, period, peer-feed and model ablations are diagnostic; publish all results with no post-test tuning.
