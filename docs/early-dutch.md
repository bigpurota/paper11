# Could the Dutch lender see the decline before a full year?

Yes, by the November 2023 forecast there were markedly fewer formal defaults in the first six months of newer Dutch loans. Using the fixed conversion from six-month to year-one events flagged a risk below both historical rates in seven of eight forecasts. Six of those warnings were correct. The available Dutch archive gives one dated snapshot of ordinary collections and arrears after these forecast dates. It cannot establish whether missed first instalments would have given the same warning sooner. ## What was measured at issue

We kept the original eight decisions at the start of October 2023 through May 2024. Their future books contain the first 25 funded loans arriving thirteen months later. The outcome is the share of their original principal associated with a recorded default in the first year. In each forecast the two original historical rates are based on loans issued 13–24 months earlier. The newer signal uses only six-month events among loans issued 7–12 months earlier; even the last loan in the latest calendar month has fully aged six months before the forecast. A six-month event is a formal default dated on or before the six-calendar-month anniversary of issue. A missed instalment need not qualify. [Original targets and date rules](dutch-frequency.md) · [Versioned May 2024 loan tape, including default dates](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/marcobeyer/bondora-loans/versions/24)

The fraction of past year-one defaults already seen by month six, h, converts the early frequency into an annual-risk estimate. We use the local estimate if there are at least five year-one defaults and one by month six. All eight Dutch dates meet these conditions. Ten prior loans anchor the recent early-event rate to h times the old local annual forecast. The two original benchmarks use the November 2026 workbook, which contains revised origination counts. The contemporaneous information claim assumes that a recorded default date was visible to the lender when it occurred. Reporting delays or corrections between that date and the archive cut cannot be recovered. The October comparison still included the October 2022 launch cohort in its young sample. That cohort moved into the mature calibration window in November. Its replacement in the younger window helped lower the early forecast. From November through May the forecasts fell below both benchmarks. The rule missed October's decline and issued a false warning in May. Across all eight forecasts the averages were **7.17% early, 27.19% local, 13.36% broader and 4.79% realized**. Principal-weighted Brier losses, giving the eight books equal weight, were 0.05091, 0.09771 and 0.05285 respectively. These are descriptive results on the cases that prompted the study.

The historical editions disagree slightly. November's original benchmark used 245 mature Dutch loans and 73 flags; the older tape has 243 loans and 72 dated year-one defaults. Recomputing both historical benchmarks and the early forecast wholly on the 2024 tape yields a mean early forecast of 7.169%, the same seven warnings and identical lending actions under both payoff scenarios. Individual loans cannot be linked reliably across these two editions because the new workbook omits the legacy IDs. [Counts and same-tape check](../results/early_dutch_episodes.csv)

## Why a correct warning can still give the wrong probability

The method assumes that the share of eventual defaults occurring early transfers to newer loans. The November case checks this assumption on the exact same 442 loans. At the forecast date, 25 had defaulted by six months. By the May 2024 archive date all 442 had finished a year, and 86 had defaulted. Only **25/86 (29.1%)** of their year-one defaults arrived by six months. The mature-converter cohort had **52/72 (72.2%)**. The formula therefore forecast 8.35% while these younger borrowers' own year-one rate reached 19.46%.

The eight early forecasts were also compared retrospectively with the annual flags for their respective younger origin windows in the revised workbook. The later count rates averaged **14.11%**, moving from 22.69% to 7.33%. The early rule underestimated seven of the eight younger cohorts. Their mean absolute error was 7.39 percentage points, compared with 5.36 points for the broader historical rate. Revisions changed the number of loans in each of these windows; the CSV records both denominators. The closer average prediction of the distant first-25 books reflects risk continuing to fall after the early signal. ## What can actual early payments and arrears tell us?

The workbook supplies a date for **full early repayment**. It permits a genuine day-90 cash marker on every forecast date. We calibrated annual risk separately for mature loans with and without this marker, then substituted its frequency among loans issued 4–9 months earlier. Only 1–38 mature marked loans were available per date. None of these marked mature loans defaulted in year one. The prevalence of full early repayment moved too little to explain the change in risk. This forecast averaged **27.29%** and warned on zero dates. Separately, the legacy Dutch tape recorded zero formal defaults within 90 days across the eligible young and mature windows. Ordinary overdue instalments can precede formal default. [Dated payment inputs](../results/early_dutch_episodes.csv) · [Bondora's description of monthly loan repayments](https://help.bondora.nl/hc/en-nl/articles/12686990936850-How-do-I-repay-my-loan)

The **24 May 2024** snapshot provides a direct check on early servicing. The 821 Dutch loans aged 60–89 days had all passed their first scheduled payment date. **817 had made a positive principal or interest/penalty payment, four had paid nothing, and five were at least 30 days past due on principal.** The four with zero receipts were all at least 30 days overdue. One borrower had paid something and still had 30-day arrears. A missing arrears-duration field coincided with zero overdue principal on all 793 loans with a missing duration. Positive cash to date does not establish the timing or completeness of the first instalment. The older tape records the first *scheduled* date and the last observed payment date.

This snapshot became available after the eight forecast dates. The latest workbook's debt start date changes when a borrower cures arrears, and its totals include subsequent repayments. Using either field to reconstruct earlier first-instalment states would introduce future information. For the same origination dates the revised workbook contains 828 loans and 56 year-one defaults (6.76%), against 821 loans in the servicing snapshot. These counts describe a dated cohort comparison. They do not measure the probability of annual default conditional on a particular borrower's recorded arrears. ## Lending consequences

At the 18.18% scenario cutoff the early rule would fund seven books. Local frequency would fund zero and the broader rate all eight. Funding the seven additional books vs local gives **+€25,329.20** on 175 originally financed loans at the assumed +10%/−45% margins. Passing on October's book costs **€4,425.20** relative to the broader policy. At a 5.66% cutoff the rule would fund five books that both historical policies would decline; their scenario difference was **−€82.16** on 125 loans. The archive records historical funded portfolios, while interest, recovery and other bank costs are represented here by assumptions. [All eight policy contrasts](../results/early_dutch_policy.csv)
