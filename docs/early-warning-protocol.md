# Can payment trouble reveal a change in risk before twelve months?

The eight Bondora Netherlands forecasts are known discovery cases. This protocol fixes the rule and a new external test before inspecting the Czech repayment outcomes. We selected the external archive by availability of dates, monthly instalments and a full year of follow-up. The test includes every nonempty region–month with an eligible loan issued from January 1996 through December 1997. An origination-only check counted 313 loans in 143 books over these 24 months. No model, threshold or test period will be reselected after scoring. [Original financial database, Czech Technical University](https://relational.fel.cvut.cz/dataset/Financial) · [Pinned copy of original files and field dictionary](https://github.com/jlacko/berka-dataset/tree/77e9972a1ead107e5f42a91dd989d587bff055a4)

## One conversion rule

At the beginning of month t, L is a local first-year event frequency and G the frequency in other areas of the same lender. Use (events+½)/(loans+1) for each. The mature history contains origin months t−24 through t−13. Let Y be the number of events by twelve months and E the number that had already occurred by six, with E a subset of Y. Estimate the fraction of year-one events already visible at six months as h=(E+½)/(Y+1). Use the local mature history if Y≥5 and E≥1. Otherwise use all areas of the lender over the same dates under the same support condition. If neither source qualifies, keep L.

In the younger local cohorts from t−12 through t−7 inclusive, count observed six-month events e among n loans. Forecast p by dividing (e + 10hL)/(n+10) by h and clipping to [0,1]. If n=0, keep L. The ten pseudo-observations anchor a small new sample to the old local forecast. The bank flags overprediction by both histories precisely when p < min(L,G), with numerical tolerance 10⁻¹². That flag predicts the direction of the error and is evaluated separately from probability loss. A change in the timing or definition of debt collection can break the conversion even if the next borrowers' ultimate risk is unchanged.

A secondary check uses an observable three-month payment indicator A. In the same local mature history estimate annual risk separately for A and its complement using half-count smoothing. Estimate the prevalence of A in younger local origin months t−9 through t−4; shrink it toward (old A+½)/(old n+1) with weight 10/(young n+10). Average the two conditional risks with this estimated prevalence. If either mature group or the younger sample is empty, use L. Indicators differ by source as described below. This is a secondary diagnostic, and cannot replace the prespecified six-month model in the primary claim.

## The two ledgers and their clocks

* **Bondora.** Keep the original eight October 2023–May 2024 issues and their later first-25 portfolio targets (origin t+13, defaulted principal share), including the original L and G. Calibrate the timing of formal default from the 24 May 2024 archive using only dated events through six or twelve calendar-month anniversaries on eligible cohorts. Check old-versus-new coverage because the two archive vintages can differ. Also report defaults by 90 days. For the secondary check use dated full early payoff by day 90 from the November 2026 workbook. Mature flags use only fully exposed vintages. The sole contemporary May 24, 2024 snapshot of cash received and arrears can describe young borrowers on that specific date. Its balances or ongoing arrears cannot enter earlier forecasts. Counts of paid-in-full loans and ordinary scheduled instalment payments have different meanings. [Bondora public workbook](https://goandgrow.eu/en/public-statistics/) · [Dutch collection procedure](https://help.bondora.nl/hc/en-nl/articles/12686711638034-What-is-the-debt-collection-procedure-for-active-loans)
* **Czech bank.** One anonymized bank, with local area defined by the account's region. For each issued loan count actual debit transactions tagged UVER from the day after origination through the end of the k-th full calendar month after origination, including eligible payments during the remainder of the origination month. At month end k a loan has a two-instalment shortfall if total tagged repayments are at most (k−2) times the contractual monthly repayment, allowing 0.005 CZK rounding tolerance. A year-one event means such a shortfall occurred at least once for k=2,…,12. The six-month event uses k=2,…,6. The three-month marker is at least one whole instalment short at the close of month three. This measures ledger shortfall and can differ from a legal default or an unpaid calendar due date. Describe negative post-debit balances in early collections. Test loans have twelve complete follow-up months by 31 December 1998. Only dated payments on older loans may affect forecasts. The target is the new book issued in t, so the forecast lead differs from the Dutch legacy exercise. Test L, G, the primary forecast and the three-month diagnostic on the same books with nonempty local and other-area history. Disclose exclusions.

## Forecast versus decision

Give each region–month equal weight in the primary Brier loss, averaging squared errors on individual loan flags within a book. Compare L minus p and G minus p using 2,500 paired circular calendar-block resamples (four months per block, seed 110618, all regions aligned). Confirmation of a predictive improvement over both benchmarks requires both 95% lower bounds above zero. Report directional flags, realized rate below both, true and false flags, and the sample base rate. Examine the three-month estimator as a secondary comparison.

A book is issued if its estimated risk is strictly below g/(g−b). Keep both payoff scenarios from the previous test, g=+10%, b=−45%, and g=+3%, b=−50%, as percentages of original principal on non-event and event. Report switches in both directions and signed payoff differences versus both baselines on funded books. A reconstructed shortfall is a proxy with an assumed loss. The comparison cannot identify an actual change in underwriting or bank profit. Publish unfavorable results and all denominators.
